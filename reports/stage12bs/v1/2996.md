We are given a circle with center $P$, and segment $AB$ is a diameter of length 4 cm. Point $C$ lies on the circle, and $BC = 2$ cm. We are to find the **area of the shaded region**, which is the area of the circle **minus** the area of triangle $ABC$.

---

### Step 1: Area of the Circle

Since $AB$ is the diameter and measures 4 cm, the radius $r = \frac{4}{2} = 2$ cm.

Area of the circle:
$$
A_{\text{circle}} = \pi r^2 = \pi (2)^2 = 4\pi \text{ cm}^2
$$

---

### Step 2: Area of Triangle $ABC$

Triangle $ABC$ has points $A$, $B$, and $C$ on the circle, with $AB$ as diameter.

**Key fact**: If a triangle is inscribed in a circle and one side is the diameter, then the angle opposite that side is a right angle. That is, $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$. This is known as **Thales’ Theorem**.

So, triangle $ABC$ is a **right triangle** with right angle at $C$.

We are given:

- $AB = 4$ cm (hypotenuse)
- $BC = 2$ cm (one leg)

We can find the other leg, $AC$, using the Pythagorean theorem:

$$
AC^2 + BC^2 = AB^2 \\
AC^2 + 2^2 = 4^2 \\
AC^2 + 4 = 16 \\
AC^2 = 12 \\
AC = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3} \text{ cm}
$$

Now, area of right triangle $ABC$:

$$
A_{\triangle ABC} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot AC \cdot BC = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\sqrt{3} \cdot 2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4\sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3} \text{ cm}^2
$$

---

### Step 3: Area of Shaded Region

Shaded region = Area of circle – Area of triangle $ABC$

$$
A_{\text{shaded}} = 4\pi - 2\sqrt{3} \text{ cm}^2
$$

---

### ✅ Final Answer:
$$
\boxed{4\pi - 2\sqrt{3}} \text{ cm}^2
