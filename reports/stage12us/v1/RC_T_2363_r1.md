The small shaded area is the sector $ECD$, which has a central angle $\theta$. Since the radius of the circle is $r = 1$ (as the area will be proportional to $r^2$, and $r$ cancels out in the condition), the area of the small shaded region is $\frac{1}{2} \theta$.

The large shaded area is the triangle $ABC$. In triangle $ABC$, $\angle A$ is a right angle because $AB$ is tangent to the circle at $A$, so $CA \perp AB$. The angle at $C$ is $\theta$, and $CA = 1$ (radius). By trigonometry, $AB = \tan \theta$ (since $\tan \theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{AB}{CA}$). The area of triangle $ABC$ is $\frac{1}{2} \times AB \times CA = \frac{1}{2} \times \tan \theta \times 1 = \frac{1}{2} \tan \theta$.

For the areas to be equal:
\[
\frac{1}{2} \tan \theta = \frac{1}{2} \theta
\]
Simplifying gives:
\[
\tan \theta = \theta
\]

This equation, $\tan \theta = \theta$, is the necessary and sufficient condition for the equality of the two shaded areas.
