## Full raw response

We are given triangle $ \triangle ABC $, with right angle at $ C $, so $ \angle C = 90^\circ $. Also, $ \angle A = 20^\circ $.

---

**Step 1: Find $ \angle ABC $.**

In any triangle, the sum of interior angles is $ 180^\circ $. So,

$$
\angle ABC = 180^\circ - \angle A - \angle C = 180^\circ - 20^\circ - 90^\circ = 70^\circ.
$$

---

**Step 2: Since $ BD $ is the angle bisector of $ \angle ABC $,**

it splits $ \angle ABC = 70^\circ $ into two equal parts:

$$
\angle ABD = \angle DBC = \frac{70^\circ}{2} = 35^\circ.
$$

---

**Step 3: Now consider triangle $ \triangle BDC $.**

We know:

- $ \angle C = 90^\circ $ (given, right angle at C),
- $ \angle DBC = 35^\circ $ (from step 2).

So, in triangle $ \triangle BDC $, the sum of angles is $ 180^\circ $, so:

$$
\angle BDC = 180^\circ - \angle DBC - \angle C = 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 90^\circ = 55^\circ.
$$

---

**Final Answer: $ \boxed{55^\circ} $**

✅ Correct option: **D. $ 55^\circ $**
