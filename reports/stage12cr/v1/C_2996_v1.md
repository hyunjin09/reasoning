## Input

Segment $AB$ measures 4 cm and is a diameter of circle $P$. In triangle $ABC$, point $C$ is on circle $P$ and $BC = 2$ cm. What is the area of the shaded region? Let's think step by step.

## Full raw response

We are given a circle with center $P$, and segment $AB$ is a diameter of length 4 cm. Point $C$ lies on the circle, and $BC = 2$ cm. We are to find the area of the shaded region.

---

### Step 1: Understand the diagram

The shaded region is the part of the circle **outside** triangle $ABC$. So:

> **Shaded Area = Area of Circle - Area of Triangle ABC**

---

### Step 2: Find the radius of the circle

Since $AB$ is the diameter and measures 4 cm, the radius $r$ is:

$$
r = \frac{4}{2} = 2 \text{ cm}
$$

---

### Step 3: Area of the circle

$$
\text{Area}_{\text{circle}} = \pi r^2 = \pi (2)^2 = 4\pi \text{ cm}^2
$$

---

### Step 4: Area of triangle $ABC$

We know:

- $AB = 4$ cm (diameter)
- $BC = 2$ cm
- $C$ is on the circle → so $\angle ACB$ is an **inscribed angle** subtending the diameter $AB$.

→ **Important theorem**: An inscribed angle that subtends a diameter is a **right angle**.

So, $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$. Therefore, triangle $ABC$ is a **right triangle** with right angle at $C$.

Thus, we can use the formula for area of a right triangle:

$$
\text{Area}_{\triangle ABC} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{leg}_1 \times \text{leg}_2
$$

But we only know one leg: $BC = 2$ cm. We need the other leg, $AC$.

Alternatively, since it’s a right triangle with hypotenuse $AB = 4$ cm, and one leg $BC = 2$ cm, we can use the Pythagorean Theorem to find $AC$:

$$
AB^2 = AC^2 + BC^2 \\
4^2 = AC^2 + 2^2 \\
16 = AC^2 + 4 \\
AC^2 = 12 \\
AC = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3} \text{ cm}
$$

Now compute area:

$$
\text{Area}_{\triangle ABC} = \frac{1}{2} \times AC \times BC = \frac{1}{2} \times 2\sqrt{3} \times 2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 4\sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3} \text{ cm}^2
$$

---

### Step 5: Shaded area = Circle area - Triangle area

$$
\text{Shaded Area} = 4\pi - 2\sqrt{3} \text{ cm}^2
$$

---

### ✅ Final Answer:

$$
\boxed{4\pi - 2\sqrt{3}} \text{ cm}^2
$$
