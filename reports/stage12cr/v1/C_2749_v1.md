## Input

In the diagram below, a diameter of each of the two smaller circles is a radius of the larger circle. If the two smaller circles have a combined area of $1$ square unit, then what is the area of the shaded region, in square units?

Options:
A. $\frac{1}{4}$
B. $\frac{1}{3}$
C. $\frac{1}{2}$
D. $1$
E. $\frac{\pi}{2}$ Let's think step by step.

## Full raw response

We are given a diagram with:

- A large circle.
- Two smaller circles inside it, each tangent to the large circle and to each other.
- The **diameter** of each smaller circle is equal to the **radius** of the large circle.

Also, the **combined area** of the two smaller circles is 1 square unit.

We are to find the **area of the shaded region**, which is the area of the large circle **minus** the areas of the two smaller circles.

---

### Step 1: Let’s assign variables.

Let the **radius of each small circle** be $ r $.

Then, the **diameter** of each small circle is $ 2r $.

According to the problem, this diameter is equal to the **radius** of the large circle.

So, the **radius of the large circle** is $ R = 2r $.

---

### Step 2: Area of the two small circles.

Each small circle has area: $ \pi r^2 $

Two of them: $ 2 \cdot \pi r^2 = 2\pi r^2 $

We’re told this equals 1:

$$
2\pi r^2 = 1
\quad \Rightarrow \quad
\pi r^2 = \frac{1}{2}
$$

---

### Step 3: Area of the large circle.

Radius of large circle is $ R = 2r $, so its area is:

$$
A_{\text{large}} = \pi R^2 = \pi (2r)^2 = \pi \cdot 4r^2 = 4\pi r^2
$$

But from above, we know $ \pi r^2 = \frac{1}{2} $, so:

$$
4\pi r^2 = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 2
$$

---

### Step 4: Shaded area = Area of large circle minus area of two small circles.

Shaded area = $ 2 - 1 = 1 $

---

### ✅ Final Answer: **D. $1$**
