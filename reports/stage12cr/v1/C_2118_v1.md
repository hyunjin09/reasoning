## Input

Each of the small circles in the figure has radius one. The innermost circle is tangent to the six circles that surround it, and each of those circles is tangent to the large circle and to its small-circle neighbors. Find the area of the shaded region.

Options:
A. $\pi$
B. $1.5\pi$
C. $2\pi$
D. $3\pi$
E. $3.5\pi$ Let's think step by step.

## Full raw response

We are given a figure with one central circle, surrounded by six smaller circles, all of radius 1. The entire arrangement is inscribed in a large outer circle. We need to find the area of the **shaded region**, which is the area **inside the large circle but outside the seven small circles**.

---

### Step 1: Understand the geometry

- Each of the **six surrounding circles** has radius 1.
- The **innermost circle** (centered at the center of the figure) is also tangent to each of the six surrounding circles.
- Each of the six surrounding circles is also tangent to the **large outer circle**.
- All circles are tangent to their neighbors as described.

This is a classic **hexagonal packing** of circles around a central circle, all inside a larger circle.

---

### Step 2: Find the radius of the large outer circle

Let’s denote:

- Let $ r = 1 $ be the radius of each small circle.
- The centers of the six surrounding circles form a regular hexagon around the center.

In a regular hexagon, the distance from the center to any vertex is equal to the side length. Since each surrounding circle is tangent to its neighbors, the distance between centers of two adjacent surrounding circles is $ 2r = 2 $.

Therefore, the **distance from the center to the center of any surrounding circle** is 2.

Now, since each surrounding circle is also tangent to the large outer circle, the radius of the large circle must be equal to the distance from its center to the center of a surrounding circle, **plus** the radius of that surrounding circle.

So,

> Radius of large circle = distance from center to surrounding circle center + radius of small circle  
> $ R = 2 + 1 = 3 $

✅ So, the large circle has radius 3.

---

### Step 3: Compute areas

- Area of large circle: $ \pi R^2 = \pi \cdot 3^2 = 9\pi $
- Area of the **seven small circles**: there are 7 circles, each of radius 1 → $ 7 \cdot \pi \cdot 1^2 = 7\pi $

Wait — but hold on! Is the innermost circle included? Yes, it's one of the "small circles" and we’re told to consider all small circles. So total area of small circles is 7π.

But then the shaded region would be:

> Shaded area = Area of large circle - Area of 7 small circles  
> = $ 9\pi - 7\pi = 2\pi $

✅ That gives us answer **C. $ 2\pi $**

---

### Double-check: Is the innermost circle really radius 1?

Yes — the problem says “each of the small circles has radius one”, and the innermost one is also a small circle. So yes, 7 circles total.

Also, the tangency conditions are satisfied:

- Distance between centers of central circle and any surrounding circle = 2 (since each has radius 1 and they’re tangent) → so centers are 2 units apart.
- Distance between centers of two adjacent surrounding circles = 2 → forms regular hexagon with side 2 → correct.
- Distance from center to edge of large circle = 2 (to center of surrounding circle) + 1 (radius of surrounding circle) = 3 → correct.

All conditions satisfied.

---

### Final Answer:

$$
\boxed{2\pi}
$$

✅ Option **C**
