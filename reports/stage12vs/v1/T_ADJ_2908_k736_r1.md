We are given:

- Three **equal circles**, each with **radius $ r = 10 $ cm**.
- The **centers form an equilateral triangle**, because the circles are tangent to each other. Hence, the distance between any two centers is $ 2r = 20 $ cm.

This configuration is standard in problems involving belts around multiple equal circles.

---

### **Step 2: Components of the Belt Length**

The belt consists of two components:

#### **(a) Straight Segments**

Each straight segment of the belt is a **common external tangent** between two adjacent circles. Since the radii are equal and the centers are separated by $ 2r $, the external tangent segment length is simply:

$$
L_{\text{straight}} = \text{distance between centers} = 2r = 20 \text{ cm}
$$

There are **three** such segments (one between each pair of circles), so:

$$
\text{Total straight segment length} = 3 \times 2r = 6r = 60 \text{ cm}
$$

#### **(b) Arc Segments**

At each circle, the belt wraps along an arc that is not facing inward toward the triangle formed by the centers. To find the arc length:

- The **angle between the two radii** to the tangent points on a single circle is **$ 120^\circ $**.
- This is because the triangle formed by the centers has angles of $ 60^\circ $, and the radii to the tangent points are perpendicular to the straight segments, resulting in a central angle of $ 120^\circ $.

$$

$$

Since there are **three** circles, the total arc length is:

$$
\text{Total arc length} = 3 \cdot \frac{20\pi}{3} = 20\pi \text{ cm}
$$

Adding both components:

$$
\text{Belt length} = \text{Straight segments} + \text{Arc segments} = 6r + 2\pi r = 60 + 20\pi
