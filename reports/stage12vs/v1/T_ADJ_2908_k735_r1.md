To determine the length of the belt surrounding three equal circles of radius 10 cm, we consider both the **straight segments** and the **curved arcs**.

---

### **1. Straight Segments**

Each pair of adjacent circles is tangent (since they are mutually tangent), so the distance between their centers is $2r = 20$ cm. The belt wraps around the outside of the circles, forming a **closed loop**. Between any two adjacent circles, the **external tangent segment** forms a straight part of the belt.

The formula for the length of an **external tangent** between two circles of equal radius $r$ with centers separated by distance $d$ is:
$$
L = \sqrt{d^2 - (r_1 - r_2)^2}
$$
Since $r_1 = r_2 = r$, this simplifies to:
$$
L = \sqrt{d^2} = d
$$
Given that $d = 2r = 20$ cm, each straight segment has length $20$ cm.

There are **three such segments**, one between each pair of circles:
$$
\text{Total straight segment length} = 3 \times 20 = 60 \text{ cm}
$$

---

### **2. Curved Arcs**

The belt wraps around each circle, forming **three arcs**, one on each circle. To find the **central angle** corresponding to each arc, consider the geometry of the three centers.

- The centers form an **equilateral triangle** with side length $2r = 20$ cm.
- At each vertex of the triangle (which corresponds to the center of a circle), the **angle between the two center-to-center lines** is $60^\circ$.
- The **angle between the two radii to the tangent points** on a single circle is also $60^\circ$, because the radii are perpendicular to the tangent segments, and the angle between the tangent segments is equal to the angle of the triangle at that vertex.

This means that the **central angle** of the arc on each circle is:
$$
\theta = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ
$$

Thus, each arc spans $120^\circ$, and since there are **three circles**, the total arc length is:
$$
\text{Total arc length} = 3 \times \left(\frac{120}{360} \times 2\pi r \right) = 3 \times \frac{1}{3} \times 2\pi r = 2\pi r
$$

For $r = 10$ cm:
$$
\text{Total arc length} = 2\pi \times 10 = 20\pi \text{ cm}
$$

---

### **3. Total Belt Length**

Adding the straight and curved parts:
$$
\text{Total belt length} = 60 + 20\pi
$$

- $a = 60$
- $b = 20$
