To determine the total length of a belt drawn tightly around three equal circles of radius $ r = 10 $ cm, we analyze the geometry and decomposition of the belt into straight segments and curved arcs.

---

### **1. Geometry of the Configuration**

- The centers of the three circles form an **equilateral triangle** with side length $ 2r = 20 $ cm.
- Each pair of adjacent circles is tangent to each other (since they have the same radius), so the distance between any two centers is $ 2r $.
- The belt is drawn **externally**, forming a closed loop that tightly wraps around all three circles.

---

### **2. Straight Segments of the Belt**

Each straight segment of the belt is an **external tangent** between two adjacent circles. For two circles of equal radius $ r $, the length of the external tangent segment between them is:

$$
L = \sqrt{d^2 - (r - r)^2} = \sqrt{(2r)^2} = 2r
$$

Thus, each of the three straight segments has length $ 2r $, and the total length of the straight parts is:

$$
\text{Total straight length} = 3 \times 2r = 6r
$$

Substituting $ r = 10 $:

$$
\text{Total straight length} = 6 \times 10 = 60 \text{ cm}
$$

---

### **3. Curved Arcs of the Belt**

Each circle contributes one arc segment to the belt. Since the belt is drawn externally, it follows the **outer path** around each circle.

#### **Central Angle for Each Arc**

- At each circle, the two tangent points (where the belt touches the circle) divide the circle into an arc and the rest of the circle.
- The angle between the two radii to these tangent points is **120°**.
  - This can be derived from the geometry: the angle between the two center-to-center lines (60°) and the right angles formed by the perpendicular radii to the tangent segments.
  - Specifically, the angle between the two radii is:
    $$
    \theta = 360^\circ - 60^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ = 120^\circ
    $$

- Therefore, the central angle corresponding to the arc on each circle is $ 120^\circ $ or $ \frac{2\pi}{3} $ radians.

#### **Arc Length for One Circle**

$$
\text{Arc length per circle} = r \cdot \theta = 10 \cdot \frac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{20\pi}{3}
$$

#### **Total Arc Length for All Three Circles**

$$
\text{Total arc length} = 3 \cdot \frac{20\pi}{3} = 20\pi \text{ cm}
$$

---

### **4. Total Belt Length**

Adding the straight and curved components:

$$
\text{Total belt length} = 60 + 20\pi
$$
