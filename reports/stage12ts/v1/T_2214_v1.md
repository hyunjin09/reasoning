Given the structure of the problem (with a trapezoid $ DBCE $), and the fact that all triangles in the diagram are similar to $ \triangle ABC $, it is reasonable to assume that the **smallest triangles lie within triangle $ ADE $** — the one directly above the trapezoid $ DBCE $.

This is supported by standard geometric constructions involving isosceles triangles and similar sub-triangles formed by lines parallel to the base.

So, the area of triangle $ ADE $ is exactly equal to the total area of the 7 smallest triangles:
$$
[\triangle ADE] = 7
$$

### Step 3: Compute Area of Trapezoid $ DBCE $

We know:
$$
[\triangle ABC] = 40
$$
$$
[\triangle ADE] = 7
$$

Since $ DE \parallel BC $, the region $ DBCE $ forms a **trapezoid**, and its area is simply the difference between the areas of the large triangle and the smaller triangle:
