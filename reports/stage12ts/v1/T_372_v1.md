To determine the **minimum number of power plants** required to cover all 16 towns in the map, we need to understand the problem as a **dominating set problem** in graph theory.

---

### 🧩 Problem Restatement

We are given a **4×4 grid of towns**, where each town is connected to its immediate neighbors via roads (i.e., up, down, left, right). A **power plant** at a town covers:

- The town itself, and
- All **immediate neighboring towns** (connected directly by a road).

Our goal is to find the **smallest number of towns** (i.e., power plants) such that **every town is either a power plant or adjacent to one** — this is the **dominating set** of the 4×4 grid graph.

---

### 🔍 Key Observations

- In a **grid graph**, the **domination number** refers to the smallest number of vertices needed to dominate the entire graph.
- For a **4×4 grid**, the **domination number is known to be 5** in standard grid graphs (e.g., from computational results or graph theory literature).
- However, **this depends on the exact structure of the graph**. If the diagram is a **4×4 square grid**, then it's a **grid graph P₄ × P₄**, which has **16 vertices**.
