### Step 1: Understand the Structure

- A **right pyramid** has an apex (point $ A $) directly above the center of the square base.
- The **base is a square**, so it has four vertices and four base edges.
- There are also **four lateral edges** from the apex to each vertex of the square base.
- Since there are **8 total edges** (4 base + 4 lateral), and **all 8 edges are of equal length**, we denote this common edge length as $ L $.

Let’s define:
- Square base vertices: $ B, C, D, E $
- Apex: $ A $
- All edges $ AB = AC = AD = AE = BC = CD = DE = EB = L $

From the diagram and description, the points $ B $ and $ D $ are **opposite corners** of the square base (since $ B $ is connected to $ C $ and $ C $ to $ D $, forming two adjacent sides of the square). Therefore, the segment $ BD $ is a **diagonal** of the square base.

### Step 2: Use Geometry to Determine Triangle $ ABD $

We consider triangle $ ABD $, where:
- $ AB = L $ (lateral edge)
- $ AD = L $ (another lateral edge)
- $ BD = L\sqrt{2} $ (diagonal of the square base)

This gives us triangle $ ABD $ with:
- Two equal sides ($ AB = AD = L $)
- A base $ BD = L\sqrt{2} $

This is a **right-angled triangle**, because:

$$
AB^2 + AD^2 = L^2 + L^2 = 2L^2 = (L\sqrt{2})^2 = BD^2
$$

So, $ \angle BAD = 90^\circ $, and triangle $ ABD $ is **isosceles right-angled** at $ A $.

In such a triangle, the two base angles are equal and sum to $ 90^\circ $. Therefore:

$$
\angle ABD = \angle ADB = \frac{180^\circ - 90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ
