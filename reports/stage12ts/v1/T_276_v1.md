To solve this problem, we are given a circular diagram with several empty circles and two known values: **3** and **5**. The rule is that **each number in the circle must equal the sum of its two direct neighbors**. This is a classic linear recurrence relation problem in a circular configuration.

---

### Step 1: Understand the Structure

We assume the diagram is a **circular arrangement of 8 circles**, labeled **1 through 8** in clockwise order. The known values are:

- Circle 1 = 3  
- Circle 4 = 5  
- All other circles are unknown (denoted as $ x_2, x_3, x_5, x_6, x_7, x_8 $)

The rule is that for each circle $ x_i $, we have:
$$
x_i = x_{i-1} + x_{i+1}
$$
with indices taken modulo 8 (since it's a circle).

Rewriting this gives:
$$
x_{i+1} = x_i - x_{i-1}
$$
This is a **linear recurrence relation** with characteristic equation:
$$
r^2 = r - 1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad r^2 - r + 1 = 0
$$
The roots are complex and form a **periodic sequence** with **period 6**.

---

### Step 2: Derive the Sequence

Let’s assume the sequence starts with:
- $ x_1 = 3 $
- $ x_2 = a $

Then using the recurrence:
$$
\begin{align*}
x_3 &= x_2 - x_1 = a - 3 \\
x_4 &= x_3 - x_2 = (a - 3) - a = -3 \\
x_5 &= x_4 - x_3 = -3 - (a - 3) = -a \\
x_6 &= x_5 - x_4 = -a - (-3) = 3 - a \\
x_7 &= x_6 - x_5 = (3 - a) - (-a) = 3 \\
x_8 &= x_7 - x_6 = 3 - (3 - a) = a \\
x_9 &= x_8 - x_7 = a - 3 = x_1 \\
x_{10} &= x_9 - x_8 = (a - 3) - a = -3 = x_4
\end{align*}
$$

So the sequence **repeats every 6 terms**, and the **period is 6**.

---

### Step 3: Apply Given Values

We are told:
- $ x_1 = 3 $
- $ x_4 = 5 $

But from the recurrence:
$$
x_4 = -3
$$

This contradicts the given value of $ x_4 = 5 $.

So the assumption that such a sequence exists is **impossible** under the given conditions.

### Step 4: Generalize the Contradiction

Let’s examine the **system of equations** that arises from the rule that each number is the sum of its two neighbors.
