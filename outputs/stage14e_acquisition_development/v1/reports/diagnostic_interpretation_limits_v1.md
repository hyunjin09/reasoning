# Interpretation limits of the source-specified diagnostic targets

The T conditions are explicitly diagnostic and never deployable. Their source-informed target strings, frozen before generation, are preserved in `inference/diagnostic_target_identities.json`. They omit the requested numerical values, shading assignments, cell masks and graph adjacency lists. They nevertheless do more than replace a noun with a correct noun: they specify the representation/frame to read and sometimes warn against a known generic template.

For example, 1067's request presupposes that the rectangle's upper edge crosses the square's sides, although the original question does not state this relationship. 2702 says not to assume a standard checkerboard, and 641 instructs the reader to follow actual strands rather than treating crossings as junctions. These are source-informed qualitative hints or procedural advice. Consequently, T versus a model-selected query does **not** isolate target identity alone and is not a clean value-free target-only factorial contrast. No quantitative target-selection causal effect or oracle upper bound should be claimed from that contrast.

This limitation does not alter saved outputs, labels, denominators or the frozen method-selection criterion. No T target enters either deployable candidate, the ledger revision, or the final evaluation. T_context versus T_blind and T_GL versus T_blind still hold the exact target text fixed and therefore remain matched component comparisons within this enriched diagnostic condition. Their small-sample uncertainty and imperfect predicted crops remain explicit. The no-image condition is a helper input ablation, not a native solver's visual-access ablation.

The defensible reading is that even these source-informed requests often yielded wrong or incomplete source readouts. That supports treating reading quality as an unresolved obstacle, but it does not prove that perfect target selection is insufficient under every readout protocol. This post hoc design-limit audit prevents an overly strong mechanistic interpretation; it is not a protocol change or a basis for tuning the held-out experiment.
